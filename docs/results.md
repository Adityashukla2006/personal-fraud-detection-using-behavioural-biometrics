# Results, Phases 0 to 8

What has been measured, how, and what it does and does not show. Every number here is regenerated by
a script in `research/` or `simulator/` and committed under `research/results/`. Tier 1 is evidence
from real data or real measurement; Tier 2 comes from a simulator written by the same people as the
defence and shows only that the logic behaves as designed (architecture section 15.1). The tiers are
never merged.

---

## 1. Tier 1: the identity channel on CMU

`research/baseline_eer.py`, `research/results/tables/baseline_eer.csv`.

| Representation | Mean EER over 51 subjects |
|---|---|
| 31 raw per-key timings, scaled Manhattan (the published setup) | 9.62% |
| Published baseline, Killourhy and Maxion (2009) | 9.62% |
| 9 aggregate features, the representation the live system can use | 20.4% |

The baseline is reproduced exactly. The deployed system cannot use per-key timings, because once the
field is not a fixed password a given key position means nothing, so it profiles 9 aggregates of the
12 features `fraudcore` extracts (the other 3 measure corrections and pastes, which CMU cannot supply).
That representation doubles the error. The identity channel that is deployed is therefore weak on its
own, which is one reason it is never allowed to escalate past step-up.

## 2. Tier 1: profile poisoning (the research question)

`research/poisoning.py`, tables under `research/results/tables/poisoning/`.

**The question.** A behavioural profile must adapt or genuine drift turns into false rejection; an
adapting profile can be dragged toward an attacker. Can the proposed update policy (P3: trust gate,
weighted geometric median, displacement budget with anchoring) adapt without being poisoned?

**Protocol.** 51 CMU victims, two attackers each, five seeds per pair for the headline variants, 95%
bootstrap intervals over pairs. The victim's recorded days supply enrolment, a genuine stream and a
held-out test; the attacker injects sessions engineered to score just inside the threshold.
Impersonation is the share of the attacker's natural held-out typing the final profile accepts; false
rejection is the share of the victim's held-out later typing it rejects.

### 2.1 How the answer changed with the protocol

The first protocol (`legacy`) held the threshold at its enrolment value and let ordinary sign-ins
adapt the profile. Under it, the `combined` variant looked strong. An audit found two confounds and
each became an arm, and a further check found the deployed scorer differs again:

| Arm | Threshold | What adapts | Attacker | Session unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `legacy` | per user, frozen at enrolment | sign-ins, 1 in 10 a step-up | tau 0.6 | one repetition |
| `recalculated` | per user, recomputed at 5% FRR | as legacy | tau 0.6 | one repetition |
| `production` | as recalculated | verified step-ups only | tau 1.0 | one repetition |
| `live` | one global threshold, from the fusion model | verified step-ups only, rebuild each session | tau 1.0 | four fields pooled |

`live` is the deployed system: it has no per-user threshold, it scores a pooled four-field session,
and it adapts only on `stepup.verified`. It is the primary arm.

### 2.2 Results on the live arm

| Policy | Impersonation | False rejection under drift |
|---|---|---|
| P0 static | 46% [38, 55] | 29% |
| P1 naive EWMA | 86% [80, 90] | 19% |
| P2 trust-gated EWMA | 82% [76, 88] | 12% |
| P3, scale adapted (`baseline`) | 100% | 0% |
| P3, scale frozen, verified anchor, median budget (`combined`) | 74.5% [68, 81] | 13% |
| P3 as above, re-anchor on 3 step-ups over 3 days (`combined_quorum`, adopted) | 74.5% [68, 81] | 13% |

What this shows:

1. **Naive adaptation is badly poisonable** (P1: 46% to 86%), and a static profile rejects 29% of
   genuine later typing. The tradeoff the project set out to study is real.
2. **Adapting the scale is catastrophic.** With the scale free, the attacker inflates it until every
   session looks normal: P3 accepts the attacker every time.
3. **With the scale frozen, P3 beats P2 by about 8 points,** with overlapping intervals. It does not
   approach static-profile security.
4. **The displacement budget does not bind in the deployed configuration.** The budget sweep is flat
   from 0.25x to 8x. Every adapting session is a step-up, and step-ups re-anchor; with the quorum the
   anchor still moves every few sessions, each time by less than one budget.
5. **The re-anchor quorum works mechanically but changes no outcome here.** It cuts re-anchors from
   96 to 16 per run and leaves impersonation unchanged. This was predicted in the pre-registration:
   at ten logins a week, three step-ups on three days assemble within days. In the sign-in arms,
   where genuine step-ups are rare, it lowers impersonation slightly (74.4% to 72.6% under
   `recalculated`, intervals overlapping).
6. **Adaptation costs security even without an attacker.** When none of the attacker's sessions pass
   a step-up, P3 still ends at 60% impersonation against P0's 46%: adapting to genuine drift loosens
   the profile. An attacker passing every step-up adds about 15 points on top.

| Attacker sessions passing step-up | P2 | P3 (`combined_quorum`) |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | 60% | 60% |
| 10% | 64% | 62% |
| 25% | 68% | 64% |
| 50% | 75% | 68% |
| 100% | 82% | 74.5% |

### 2.3 What was adopted, and why

`models.json` now carries `combined_quorum`: frozen scale, verified anchor, centre budget at the median
of genuine drift between consecutive recording days measured in pooled sessions (1.17), and the
three-step-ups-over-three-days quorum. It is equal to or better than the previous live configuration on
every arm, and it corrects a unit error: the previous budget was calibrated on single repetitions,
while live profiles are built from pooled sessions. The quorum is kept because it is principled and
costs nothing, not because it was shown to protect.

### 2.4 The time axis

CMU records eight sittings per subject, on separate days, of one ten-character password. The genuine
stream is four of them. To let the 30-day quiet re-anchor ever fire, the experiment stretches those
four days over a simulated 140 days (`stretched`). On the `recorded` calendar, where the stream spans
the four days it was typed on, `combined_quorum` P3 reaches 67% impersonation rather than 75%, because
the quorum can form only once. Two limits follow and are not fixable with this dataset: CMU drift is
mostly practice on one password over days, not months of real behavioural change, and the
calibrated budget is "one recording-day gap" of drift, whose real duration CMU does not record.

### 2.5 What is proven and what is not

Proven on real typing: naive adaptation is poisonable; a static profile does not tolerate drift;
adapting the scale must be forbidden; the live trigger means an attacker without the authenticator
never reaches adaptation (0 profiles changed in the live Phase 8 run).

Not shown: that the robust estimator and displacement budget add meaningful protection against an
attacker who holds the authenticator. On the deployed configuration they do not. The defence that
works is the step-up gate itself, and the barrier it sets is possession of the passkey.

## 3. Tier 1: the deployed pipeline

`simulator/measure_latency.py`, `simulator/measure_cost.py`,
`research/results/tables/phase8_latency.csv`, `phase8_cost.csv`.

| Measure | p50 | p95 |
|---|---|---|
| Warm round trip | 145 ms | 229 ms |
| Warm handler | 24 ms | 29 ms |
| Warm DynamoDB read | 3.8 ms | 4.8 ms |
| Warm confirmation round trip (starts the workflow) | 172 ms | 392 ms |
| Cold round trip | 1,162 ms | 1,500 ms |

The p95 target of 200 ms is missed on the round trip (229 ms) and met on the handler; most of the
round trip is API Gateway and the network from the measuring client. Cold starts are reported, not
masked.

Measured cost is **$0.23 per thousand sessions**, from real invocation counts over 250 simulated
sessions and 1,000 API calls. Step Functions state transitions are 46% of it.

## 4. Tier 2: attack detection on the live endpoint

`simulator/run_attacks.py`, `research/results/tables/phase8_detection.csv`; 10 CMU victims, 50
sessions per class, fusion weights fitted on 28 other subjects.

| Class | Detected | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Account takeover | 100% | block |
| Bot (constant interval) | 100% | step-up |
| Replay (protocol nonce disabled) | 100% | step-up |
| Poisoning session | 36% | step-up |
| Genuine | 16% friction | step-up |

Bot and replay stop at step-up by design: they are behavioural signals and the friction ceiling
forbids a behavioural channel alone from restricting or blocking. The 100% rates describe separable
synthetic classes and should not be read as field detection rates.

Low-and-slow siphoning (Phase 9) is not reported here: Phase 9 was paused until this write-up, and
its replay restricted every genuine control on day one, which is unexplained (see section 5).

## 5. Open issues

1. **Poisoning defence beyond the gate.** A rule that would make the budget bind against an
   authenticator holder has not been found. Candidates: an anchor that moves only on sessions separated
   by a minimum interval, or a cap on total anchor movement per 30 days.
2. **The live global threshold is loose.** Before any poisoning, 46% of an impostor's pooled sessions
   pass behaviour without a step-up. This is the fusion model's calibration on synthetic sessions, and
   it is the largest single weakness in the deployed identity channel.
3. **A new payee's alert depends on whether the batch layer has seen it.** A new, unverified payee
   scores 2.0 on the payee channel, and the channel's confidence is 0.5 plus half the freshness of the
   payee's batch risk item. With no batch item, confidence is 0.5 and the representation (1.67) stays
   below the alert level of 2. With any fresh batch item, however benign (a risk of 0.10 is enough),
   confidence approaches 1, the representation reaches 3.3, and the channel alerts. Two alerting
   channels corroborate a block, so a genuine user whose typing has drifted is blocked for paying a
   new payee that someone else has recently paid, and only stepped up for one nobody has. The Phase 5
   integration test failed this way once the nightly aggregator had scored its test payees, and it is
   the most likely cause of the Phase 9 controls being restricted on day one (the replay cleaned up its
   decisions, so that link is inferred, not shown). The novelty and verification terms are live facts
   and arguably should not take their confidence from the batch layer's freshness. Changing that
   changes the deployed model, so it is left as a decision.
